<h2 style="color: #245ab3;">The Bus Stop Analogy</h2>
Date-08/09/2026
<p>
It's been months since I wrote a blog. I'll write two today: the Bus Stop Analogy, and a technical piece.
</p>
<p>
<b>Context:</b> I take the bus every day from the Delhi bus stand to college. The unique thing about Delhi buses is that they dgaf. They don't wait for people to board. They stop only as long as boarded passengers get off, and that's it. You have to make your way in by then. The bus stop is 100m from my home, and I can see it as soon as I step out.
</p>
<p>
<b>Story:</b> As I step out I see buses stopping in front of me (100–200m) for a brief 3–4 seconds, then moving on. I tried catching those buses by running at them, waving my hands, but it was of no use. This happened for 3–4 days. I step out, see a bus stopping 100m away, I run at it, the driver sees me but still decides to move. For a few days I thought: had I been early by a minute I might have caught that bus. The thing that makes this funnier is that buses arrive at a 3–4 minute gap. So even if I miss a bus that stopped right in front of me, I wouldn't wait more than 3–4 minutes before the next one arrives, and by that time I'd already be at the stop.
</p>
<p>
<b>Realisation:</b> I get out, see a bus stopping, passing by, I smile. These buses are those events in life that you "miss". You can't do a thing about a missed event, or one that's about to be missed, except smile at it. That's life. It's stupid to cry over a missed bus. They come, they go, they wait for none. Nothing waits for us. Surprisingly the next event is on the way in 3–4 minutes, and you're at the right spot to catch it. Cheers!
</p>
